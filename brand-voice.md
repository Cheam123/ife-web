# Ronda Brand Voice Guide

This guide ensures Ronda’s voice is clear, consistent, and respectful of our users’ time and intelligence across all products and communications.

---

## 1. Personality

Our personality is built on being the helpful, reliable partner to the busy people who keep Malaysia’s food and beverage outlets running.

### Reliable & Routine-Focused
We focus on the steady rhythm of a successful day. We talk about the value of doing the rounds, and the satisfaction of a routine executed well.
* **We are:** Consistent, steady, predictable, calm.
* **We are not:** Unpredictable, hyperactive, erratic, dull.
* **Example:** *"Ready for your rounds? Today’s routine is in the Visits tab."*

### Supportive & Empathetic
We understand our users are busy, often on their feet, and juggling multiple tasks. We speak with an understanding of their reality, offering support instead of creating pressure.
* **We are:** Encouraging, understanding, practical, human.
* **We are not:** Condescending, overly formal, bossy, cold.
* **Example:** *"We’ve pre-filled the visit form to save you time."*

### Simple & Clear
Our primary language is English, but it’s often a second language. We use plain English (CEFR B1 level) with direct sentence structures. We avoid idioms and jargon that don't travel.
* **We are:** Concise, clear, direct, easy to understand.
* **We are not:** Complex, verbose, vague, idiomatic.
* **Example:** *"Order submitted successfully."*

### Trustworthy & Transparent
Trust is the basis of our relationship. We are completely upfront about how we use data. We never use language that is sneaky, manipulative, or creates false pressure.
* **We are:** Honest, direct, transparent, respectful.
* **We are not:** Deceptive, ambiguous, pressure-filled, 'sneaky'.
* **Example:** *"We only store your GPS location when you stamp this form."*

## 2. Tone by Context

Our tone is always rooted in our personality, but we adjust the expression based on the situation and the user's emotional state.

| Context | User’s State | Desired Tone | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Push Notifications** | Busy, on-the-go. Needs info at a glance. | Brief, Urgent but Calm, Actionable. Get the core message across without causing panic. | *"Visit needed: Daily Grind SS2. Click to see details."* |
| **Empty States** | Looking for info, may be confused. | Supportive, Instructive, Positive. Explain what’s happening and guide the next step. | *"No new product suggestions today. Keep recording orders to help our AI learn."* |
| **Errors & Validation** | Frustrated, blocked from a task. | Helpful, Clear, Problem-Solving. State what went wrong and how to fix it, without blame. | *"Please complete all required fields on the service form before submitting."* |
| **Success Confirmations** | Relieved, satisfied a task is done. | Positive, Efficient, Reassuring. Confirm success quickly so they can move on. | *"Order sent. Good work on that visit!"* |
| **AI Morning Brief** | Starting their day, planning the routine. | Calm, Focus-Enabling, Personable. Summarise with clarity and a warm, supportive opening. | *"Good morning, [Name]. Here are 3 at-risk tasks to focus on today..."* |
| **AI "Why These?"** | Curious, skeptical. Needs logic for a suggestion. | Logical, Data-Driven, Simple. Explain the reason (e.g., similar outlet, order history) in clear terms. | *"Outlets like this one usually order more of this product in this season."* |
| **Legal & Privacy Pages** | Potentially concerned, cautious. | Formal but Approachable, Completely Honest, Plain English. Build trust by being clear and open. | *"We take your privacy seriously. We only record your location when you explicitly perform an action..."* |
| **Onboarding (First Time)** | Curious, possibly a bit overwhelmed. | Encouraging, Warm, Benefit-Oriented. Guide them gently, highlighting how Ronda will help. | *"Welcome! Let’s show you how to start your first round of visits."* |

## 3. Word List & Style

Our language choices reinforce our identity. We lean into the language of rounds and routine, and actively avoid words of surveillance and control.

### Preferred vs Avoid Terms

| Use This | Avoid This | Reason |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Outlet, Customer** | Lead, Prospect, Account | More customer-centric, focused on the relationship. |
| **Visit, Round** | Call, Stop, Task | Focuses on the routine and quality interaction. |
| **Stamp Location, Form Stamp** | Track, Monitor, Surveillance | We do not monitor people. We record specific actions with their consent. |
| **Routine, Schedule** | List, To-Do, Plan | Emphasises the steady, rhythmic nature of the work. |
| **Suggested Order, Recommended Products** | Upsell, AI Picks | More helpful and less like a direct sales pitch. |
| **At-Risk, Overdue** | Problem, Error, Mistake | Describes the status, not the person responsible. |
| **Record, Log** | Enter, Input | Sounds more like documenting a part of a routine. |

### Style Guide

* **Capitalisation:** Use Sentence case for all headings, labels, button text, and UI copy. Only capitalise the first word and proper nouns.
* **Numbers:** Use numerals (e.g., "3 visits", not "three visits") for readability. For large numbers, use a comma for the thousands separator (e.g., "RM 1,234.00").
* **Dates & Times:** Use a clear, unambiguous format.
  * **Date:** "28 Sep 2026" (Use 3-letter month abbreviation).
  * **Time:** "2:30 PM" (Use AM/PM).
* **Currency:** Standardise on "RM" with two decimal places (e.g., "RM 1,234.00").

## 4. UI Copy Rewrites

We apply our voice to real-world strings to make them more Ronda.

| Before | After | Why the change? |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Welcome Back To Ronda! / Sign in to continue. | **Ready for your round? Sign in.** | The first line is too long and generic. The rewrite is shorter, benefit-focused, and uses our core language. The second line is a clearer action. |
| Capture a valid location for Site location. | **Tap to stamp your current location.** | "Capture" sounds complex and slightly technical. "Valid" is not helpful for the user to understand what is wrong. "Stamp" is our preferred word and action-oriented. |
| This task will be assigned to you. | **You will start this task.** | Focuses on the user taking the action of starting, making it more active and direct than the passive "be assigned." |
| Not enough data yet. Recommendations need this outlet's profile (type, size, segment) and order history from similar outlets. | **We need more information to give you suggestions. Order history and outlet details from similar locations help us learn.** | Shorter, uses simple words. Explains the 'why' without sounding like a data science error message. |
| **Title:** 3 tasks at risk, 1 overdue <br>**Body:** T-20260928-0004 Collect overdue invoice (Daily Grind SS2) - Due within 8 h | **Title:** 3 at-risk tasks to check <br>**Body:** Complete your visit to Daily Grind SS2 to collect the overdue invoice. | "Tasks at risk" and "1 overdue" is a lot for a notification. The title focuses on what to check. The body gives the core action (visit Daily Grind SS2 for the invoice) in clear language. Removed the ticket number. |
| You do not have permission to add tasks. | **Only your manager can add a task. Check with them.** | The original is hostile and puts the blame on the user ("you do not have permission"). The rewrite explains who can do it, providing a clear path forward. |
| Lead not found. | **We could not find this outlet.** | "Lead" is an avoid word. "Outlet" is our preferred word. "Not found" is passive; "could not find" is simpler and more conversational. |
| Location permission was denied. Allow location for this site in the browser, then tap Retry. | **We could not check your location. Please check your browser’s permission for this site and retry.** | Avoids the negative "denied." Focuses on what went wrong and how to fix it in simpler terms ("check your browser's permission" vs "allow location"). |

## 5. Feature Names

We provide names that are simple, describe the function, and fit the voice.

| Feature | Option 1 | Option 2 | Our Pick & Why |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **AI morning brief** | Morning Round-Up | Day’s Routine | **Morning Round-Up:** Connects to both "morning brief" and our core concept of "rounds," sounding less like a rigid instruction than "Day’s Routine." |
| **Product suggestions tab** | Product Hub | Suggested Orders | **Suggested Orders:** Directly states what's in the tab and is focus-oriented. "Hub" is a bit of a cliché. |
| **At-risk alert** | Focus Alert | Task Update | **Focus Alert:** Clearly points to something needing attention without being overly dramatic. "Task Update" is too generic. |
| **GPS location stamp** | Location Stamp | Check-In Stamp | **Location Stamp:** Short, clear, and uses our preferred word ("stamp") to describe the action. "Check-In Stamp" is a good second choice but is slightly longer. |

## 8. AI Voice Block

This block can be pasted into the system prompts for our AI features to ensure their output aligns with Ronda’s voice.

```text
You are Ronda, a simple, reliable, and helpful assistant for food & beverage field teams. Your primary audience is busy users in Malaysia, so write with a direct and encouraging tone. Use plain, easy-to-understand English (CEFR B1 level). For the Morning Brief, summarize key insights like at-risk tasks with clarity and focus. For Product Suggestions, provide simple, data-driven explanations (e.g., "Outlet type orders more of this" or "Similar outlets are buying this product"). Never be preachy, overly formal, or use idioms. Build trust with every word. Be transparent about data usage and focus on supporting the user in their daily routine.
```
